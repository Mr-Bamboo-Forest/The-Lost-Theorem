The Lore of The Lost Theorem

The history of eleven mathematical zones and the theorem that sealed them

The Lost Theorem is more than a puzzle game. It is a mathematical adventure game built around a rich world of NPCs, ancient ruins, zombie spawners, and a mystery that spans eleven distinct zones. This page explores the lore, history, and characters that give each zone its identity, and explains why mathematics sits at the heart of everything.

The Founding Theorem

Long before the eleven zones were named, a single theorem held them together. Known simply as The Theorem, it was not a formula but a principle: that every branch of mathematics is connected, that no idea exists in isolation, and that the universe of numbers forms a continuous whole.

The Theorem was discovered by a mathematician known only as The Architect, who spent decades travelling between disciplines, from the geometry of Euclid Eutopia to the probability distributions of Probability Hollow, finding the hidden threads that linked them all. When The Architect finally wrote down the proof, the zones stabilised. Trade routes opened. Knowledge spread. Characters who had once lived only in their own mathematical domain began to speak with scholars from others.

"Every integral hides a derivative. Every probability hides a structure. The theorem does not unify mathematics. It reveals that mathematics was always unified."

Then something happened. Historians call it the Sealing. The Theorem was lost, and the zones began to drift apart. Without the connecting proof, each zone turned inward. Ancient ruins stopped making sense. The dead began to walk. And seventy-seven Artefacts, each one a fragment of the original Theorem's evidence, were scattered across the world.

The Eleven Zones

Algebra Aces — The Starting Zone

A world of ancient ruins and buried archives at the centre of everything, Algebra Aces is where the journey begins. The Ruins Sage solves cubic polynomials with the patience of someone who has been doing it for decades. The Matrix Keeper maintains a grid of simultaneous equations on the walls of a stone chamber. Every path outward from the centre leads through this zone first.

"The polynomial yields to those who know one root. Factor it out. The quadratic will follow." — Ruins Sage

The Integral Plains

A vast grassland crossed by slow rivers, the Integral Plains were once the heartland of mathematical commerce. The Archivist catalogues every indefinite integral in leather-bound volumes. The River Keeper guards the definite integral of the river's flow. The Shrine Keeper tends a mossy altar where offerings were always areas beneath parabolas.

"The Fundamental Theorem connects what was separated. Differentiation and integration are inverse operations. This stone carries the proof." — Shrine Keeper

Euclid Eutopia

A coastal zone of lighthouses, docks, and tidal ruins, Euclid Eutopia was built by geometers who measured everything before they built it. The Lighthouse Keeper calculated the light's reach using only angles and shadow. The Merchant prices by area, always inscribing a circle before quoting. The City Surveyor mapped every lot using similarity and scale.

Probability Hollow

A twilight zone of misty hollows and whispered calculations, Probability Hollow is where uncertainty became a discipline. The Oracle does not claim to see the future — she calculates its likelihood. The Gambler has spent twenty years computing expected values without ever actually gambling. The Tree Builder constructs probability trees from raw outcomes, leaves labelled with exact fractions.

Trig Peaks

A zone of mountain passes and coastal watchtowers, Trig Peaks is where every distance was measured before it was walked. The Climber applies the sine rule to plan every ascent. The Navigator uses the cosine rule when no right angle exists. The Seismologist reads sinusoidal waves from underground sensors and can name the amplitude, period, and phase shift of any tremor.

Derivation City

A zone of workshops, laboratories, and junction stations, Derivation City is where rates of change became a science. The Chain Rule Explorer decomposes composite functions with mechanical precision. The Chemist applies the product rule to reaction rates. The Rail Planner uses implicit differentiation to calculate every curve in the network.

"Not all curves can be written as y = f(x). Differentiate both sides implicitly with respect to x." — Rail Planner

The Vector Wastelands

A flat, hostile zone mapped entirely in vectors, the Wastelands have no landmarks — only directions and magnitudes. The Vector Wanderer maps the zone using position vectors and displacement. The Dot Product Sage found the angle between every pair of ruined walls. The Cross Guardian's gate opens only perpendicular to both approach vectors.

Exponential Depths

Underground caverns where everything grows or decays at a rate proportional to itself. The Growth Keeper models populations and compound interest without approximation. The Logarithm Hermit solves every exponential by taking logs. The Decay Warden tracks radioactive half-lives with silent precision.

The Combinatorial Archives

A vast library of arrangements, selections, and structures. The Arrangement Sage has counted every permutation of every set in the Archives. The Selection Scholar chairs committees with mathematical precision. The Derangement Master counts the permutations where nothing is in its correct position — and has never once been in the wrong place himself.

The Null Space

The most abstract and mysterious of the eleven zones, the Null Space is where complex numbers live. Its NPCs include The Complex Guide, The Modulus Keeper, De Moivre's Shadow, The Conjugate Twin, and The Locus Painter. The zone itself does not fully conform to the laws of the other ten.

"The Null Space is not the absence of mathematics. It is the space where mathematics becomes something more than itself." — Inscription above the entrance

Statistical Archipelago

A chain of islands in the furthest south, each devoted to a different statistical discipline. The Data Collector has measured everything. The Hypothesis Judge has never made a Type I error. The Confidence Sage builds interval estimates around every claim before speaking it aloud.

The Artefacts

Seventy-seven Artefacts are scattered across the eleven zones, one per NPC encounter. Each Artefact is a physical object that once belonged to a scholar, surveyor, keeper, or wanderer, and each carries a fragment of the Lost Theorem's evidence. Some also grant gameplay bonuses: extra lives, timed shields, or both.

Collect all seventy-seven and the outlines of the Lost Theorem begin to emerge.

The Zombie Awakening

When the Theorem was lost, something else happened. The mathematical structures that kept the dead in place became inconsistent. Purple skull spawners appeared throughout the zones, endlessly producing the Awakened — zombies who path slowly but relentlessly toward any living mathematician. They cannot be permanently stopped; only the recovery of all Artefacts will restore the Theorem and end the Awakening.

Questions pause the Awakened. They freeze mid-step while a scholar answers. This is the only mercy the Null Space grants.

Keywords and Context

The Lost Theorem is a free browser-based puzzle game, maths adventure game, and educational RPG for secondary and senior secondary students. It covers calculus, integration, differentiation, geometry, probability, statistics, algebra, trigonometry, vectors, complex numbers, combinatorics, and mathematical induction.
